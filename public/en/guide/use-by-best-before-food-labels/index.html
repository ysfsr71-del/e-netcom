Use By vs Best Before: Cut Food Waste Without Compromising Safety

Learn the difference between use-by and best-before dates, safer storage habits, and a practical school activity to reduce food waste.

Two dates, two different meanings

Date labels can help prevent unnecessary food waste, but only if we read them correctly. A use-by date and a best-before date do not mean the same thing. Storage instructions, whether a pack has been opened, and its condition also matter.

Use by: a safety limit

A use-by date is linked to food safety, particularly for highly perishable foods. Do not eat a food after its use-by date, even if it looks and smells normal: harmful microorganisms cannot always be detected by the senses. A broken cold chain can make food unsafe even before the printed date.

Best before: a quality guide

Best before generally refers to expected quality when a product is stored as directed. Passing this date does not automatically make food unsafe, although flavour, texture or freshness may decline. Consider the condition of correctly stored, intact products carrying a best-before label; discard food showing mould, leaks, swelling or other signs of spoilage.

Storage and opened packages

Follow the temperature and storage directions on the package. Once opened, a product may have a shorter safe storage period than the printed date suggests. Do not leave perishable foods at room temperature for extended periods. Never taste a questionable product to test its safety; follow the manufacturer's instructions and official food-safety guidance.

A five-step label check

1) Identify whether the label says use by or best before. 2) Read the date carefully. 3) Check storage requirements. 4) Check whether the pack has been opened or damaged. 5) Never eat food beyond its use-by date; assess best-before products only when storage and product condition permit.

A 30-minute classroom activity

Use fictional label cards prepared by the teacher rather than asking pupils to bring food from home. In groups, sort cards by date type and storage instruction. Discuss why smell or appearance cannot establish the safety of food past its use-by date. Do not taste samples; this is a label-literacy exercise.

A one-week waste audit

For seven days, record broad reasons for household food disposal without collecting personal information: overbuying, poor storage, date confusion or leftovers. Compare the categories at the end of the week. Report reductions only if they were actually measured; do not infer precise carbon savings from a small classroom exercise.

Mistakes to avoid

Treating best before as a strict safety deadline can increase waste; treating use by as merely a quality suggestion can create health risks. Smelling or tasting does not make an expired use-by product safe. Labelling rules vary between jurisdictions, so follow the official requirements applicable where you live.
