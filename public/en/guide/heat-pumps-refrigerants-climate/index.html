Heat pumps, refrigerants and climate claims

Understand heat pumps, refrigerant leakage and how to evaluate climate claims with evidence.

How does a heat pump work?

A heat pump transfers heat from its surroundings into a building instead of producing all its heat by burning fuel on site. Some models can also cool. Electricity use does not make the entire system automatically zero-emission: the electricity mix, insulation, weather, design and operation all matter.

Why refrigerants matter

Refrigerants vary in their global warming potential. Leaks during operation, servicing or end-of-life handling can add climate impacts if the gas is not properly recovered. Comparing electricity consumption alone therefore misses part of the life cycle.

Check both extremes

“Heat pumps cause no greenhouse gas emissions” ignores upstream electricity and potential refrigerant releases. “Refrigerants make heat pumps worse than fossil heating in every case” ignores efficiency, local electricity, leakage and other assumptions. Ask which technology, year, location, operating conditions and accounting boundaries a claim uses.

Classroom activity

Compare two advertisements for heating systems. Record the energy source, efficiency metric, refrigerant type, maintenance and recovery information, and an independent source. Write “not stated” rather than inventing missing figures.

Practical perspective

Correct sizing, building insulation, professional installation, servicing and appropriate refrigerant recovery are relevant. Do not publish a universal percentage saving without defining the comparison.
