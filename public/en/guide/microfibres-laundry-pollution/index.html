How can we reduce fibre pollution from laundry?

A practical guide to synthetic textile fibres, garment care, washing choices and evidence-aware action.

What are microfibres?

Synthetic textiles such as polyester, acrylic and nylon can shed tiny fibres during wear and washing. Some reach wastewater. Not every microfibre is plastic: cotton and wool also shed fibres, whereas synthetic fibres are made from polymers.

Why does it matter?

Wastewater treatment can capture part of the fibre load, but capture is not necessarily complete. Retained fibres may move into sewage sludge. Ecological impacts are being studied, so a precise harm figure for one laundry load would be misleading.

Wash only when needed

Avoid washing clothes automatically after every wear when hygiene and care requirements do not demand it. Follow garment labels, consider suitable lower temperatures and gentle cycles, and load the machine as recommended by its manufacturer. Do not overload it.

Keep garments in use

Choose durable clothing, repair damage, consider second-hand options and extend useful life. Fibre shedding varies with fabric construction, age and washing conditions; no fabric can be promised to shed zero fibres.

Filters and capture devices

External filters and laundry bags may capture some released fibres. Performance varies across products and test conditions; look for independent evidence. Never rinse collected lint down a drain; dispose of it according to local waste guidance.

A classroom activity

For one week, record washing frequency, programme choices and garment-care habits without collecting identifying information. Compare habits before and after the activity. Do not present this as a direct microplastic measurement: reliable quantification requires appropriate laboratory methods.

Common misconceptions

Claims that natural textiles shed no fibres, that every filter catches all microplastics or that cold washing is always appropriate are oversimplifications. Prioritise garment instructions, hygiene and verified evidence.

An action plan for today

Check the care labels of three garments, identify one item to repair and reduce unnecessary washes over the next week. Track actual washing frequency separately from any estimated resource savings. Do not invent a percentage reduction in microplastic pollution.
