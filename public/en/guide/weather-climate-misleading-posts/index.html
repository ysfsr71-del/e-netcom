Does one cold day disprove climate change?

Learn how weather differs from climate and check misleading social media claims with three practical questions.

Weather is not the same as climate

A photograph of snowfall may circulate with the caption “Where is global warming?” The claim confuses short-term weather with long-term climate. Weather describes temperature, precipitation and wind at a particular place and time. Climate concerns patterns, averages and variability over many years.

Why one photograph is not enough

Cold days can still occur in a warming world. Equally, one hot day does not establish every cause of climate change. Researchers evaluate long-term observations and multiple datasets. The IPCC concludes that human activities, primarily through greenhouse gas emissions, have unequivocally caused global warming.

Three checks before sharing

Does the post generalise from one city or day? Can you verify the photograph’s date, location and the data period? Is the claim consistent with long-term findings from NASA and the IPCC? A genuine photograph can still have a misleading caption.

Classroom activity

Compare a one-day weather forecast for your area with a long-term temperature chart. Note the date ranges and discuss which of two social media headlines more accurately reflects the evidence.
