Light Pollution and Night Ecosystems: Smarter Outdoor Lighting

How artificial light affects wildlife and energy use, with a safe school lighting audit and practical steps.

What is light pollution?

Light pollution is artificial light used in the wrong place, at the wrong time, or at excessive levels. Skyglow, glare and light spill are distinct forms. The issue goes beyond losing sight of stars: energy use and natural nighttime conditions may also be affected.

Why does wildlife need darkness?

Many species depend on darkness to forage, navigate and avoid predators. Artificial light can disorient some migrating birds and change insect behaviour. Effects vary by species, spectrum, intensity and duration; not all animals respond in the same way.

Lighting and safety belong together

The aim is not to leave paths or stairs dangerously dark. Essential visibility, emergency routes and accessibility must be maintained while unnecessary illumination is reduced. Students must never alter electrical installations; qualified staff should assess any proposed changes.

A five-question lighting audit

Is the light necessary? Does it illuminate only the intended surface? Is it brighter than required? Can a timer or sensor control it? Is a warmer colour appropriate? These questions translate the DarkSky–IES principles into a practical checklist.

A school observation activity

Students map school lighting in daylight under teacher supervision. Any after-dark observations must be carried out by authorised adults in safe areas. Record each fixture's purpose, direction, operating hours and spill. Do not disturb nesting wildlife or use camera flashes.

A seven-day action plan

Day 1: inventory; day 2: identify safety requirements; day 3: locate unnecessary light; day 4: consult teachers and facilities staff; day 5: review control options; day 6: share findings; day 7: repeat observations. School management makes implementation decisions.

How to measure progress

Compare operating hours, fixture counts and measured electricity use where available before and after changes. Do not claim precise savings without data. Record visibility and accessibility concerns as well; less light is not the only measure of success.

Common misconceptions

Not every LED installation is automatically environmentally friendly, and switching off every light is not the answer. Colour temperature alone cannot solve the problem. Need, direction, intensity, timing and colour must be considered together.
