The hidden water and energy footprint of data centres

Understand data-centre electricity demand, water withdrawal versus consumption, and how to fact-check digital footprint claims.

Digital services have physical infrastructure

Online searches, streamed videos and AI requests rely on servers in data centres. Servers use electricity; some cooling technologies consume water on-site, while electricity generation may also have an indirect water footprint.

Read the figures carefully

The International Energy Agency's Energy and AI report, published on 10 April 2025, estimates global data-centre electricity demand at about 415 TWh in 2024 and projects about 945 TWh in 2030 under its Base Case. The second figure is a scenario projection, not observed consumption. Neither figure measures litres per AI request.

Withdrawal is not consumption

Water withdrawal measures water taken from a source. Consumption concerns the portion not returned to the same local system in an available form. Cooling design, local climate, facility location and power mix affect the results.

Five checks for a viral claim

Identify the year, geography, unit and system boundary. Ask whether the figure includes on-site cooling, electricity supply or both. Check that withdrawals and consumption are distinguished, and that uncertainties and methods are disclosed. Avoid assuming one universal litres-per-request value.

Classroom activity

Compare three media claims about digital water use. Record the claim, unit, year, location, accounting boundary and primary source. Mark missing details as not stated rather than inventing numbers.

Practical choices

Keep devices in use longer where feasible, turn off unwanted video autoplay and request transparent energy and water reporting from service providers. Do not claim exact savings without evidence.
