Spotting Water Leaks at School: A Safe Observation Guide

A practical student guide to spotting visible signs of water loss, recording observations and reporting concerns without touching plumbing.

Why small leaks matter

A dripping tap or a continuously running toilet can waste water unnoticed. Students are not expected to repair plumbing: their role is to observe visible signs and report them to responsible adults.

Safety comes first

Keep away from slippery floors. If water is near electrical sockets, cables or equipment, leave the area and alert school staff immediately. Never touch pipes, valves, meters or electrical installations.

Where to observe

With a teacher, look only at clearly visible taps, the floor around sinks and the sound of a toilet that keeps running. Do not open panels, enter restricted areas or inspect hidden plumbing.

Keep an observation log

Use five columns: date, time, location, visible sign and staff member notified. Follow school privacy rules for photographs; never photograph people using toilets or other private facilities.

Report and follow up

Tell designated maintenance staff about any concern. Do not attempt to test a repair. On a later day, a teacher may help you observe from a safe public area whether the visible sign remains.

Avoid exaggerated savings claims

Drop size and dripping rate vary. Do not publish a precise litres-per-day estimate without an appropriate measurement. Authorised staff can assess water meter data.

Four-week activity

Week 1: safety briefing and log template. Week 2: supervised observation. Week 3: follow-up with staff. Week 4: review anonymised results. A responsible teacher oversees every stage.

Share useful outcomes

Without naming individuals or revealing sensitive locations, report how many signs were observed, referred to staff and resolved. Connect the activity to e-NetCoM's sustainability education.
