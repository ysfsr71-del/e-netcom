A School Library of Things: Share Before You Buy

A practical guide to lending seldom-used school equipment with safe inventory, maintenance, fair access and meaningful impact measures.

What is a library of things?

A library of things lets students borrow infrequently used equipment from a shared collection rather than requiring everyone to buy their own. It follows the familiar library model but can include magnifiers, measuring containers, binoculars and suitable activity supplies. Its goal is to avoid unnecessary purchases, not merely to sort waste.

Why can sharing help?

Environmental impacts occur before an item becomes waste: raw material extraction, manufacturing, packaging and transport all require resources. The European Environment Agency explains that sharing models can reduce demand for new products, although the outcome depends on whether borrowing genuinely replaces purchases. Do not assign an automatic carbon-saving figure to every loan.

Choose a safe starter collection

Ask students which portable, durable and easy-to-clean items they need only occasionally. Begin with low-risk equipment that can be inspected and repaired. Exclude personal hygiene items, protective safety equipment, powered cutting tools, chemicals and age-inappropriate devices. Record an item ID, condition, photo where useful and a responsible adult.

Set simple borrowing rules

A paper or digital log should record the item code, checkout date, due date and condition on return. Avoid collecting unnecessary personal information about children. Clean and inspect items between users. When something breaks, focus on repair and learning rather than punitive fees.

Safety and accessibility

Provide age guidance and clear instructions for each item. Secure shelving, store heavy objects low and inspect for sharp or damaged parts. Make the lending area wheelchair accessible and use legible, high-contrast labels. Teachers should supervise unfamiliar equipment.

Run a four-week pilot

Week one: survey needs and select items. Week two: label, inventory and plan cleaning. Week three: launch supervised lending. Week four: collect feedback and adjust. A collection of 10–15 items is a reasonable pilot, not a universal target. Keep only equipment that serves a real need.

Measure outcomes carefully

Track loans, distinct items borrowed, on-time returns, maintenance and satisfaction. An optional anonymous question—'Would you have bought this item otherwise?'—can help assess potential avoided purchases. These responses are estimates, not verified emissions reductions. Review underused items monthly.

Keep it going

Share responsibility among the school library, families and student representatives. Inspect donated items against the same safety criteria as purchased ones. Explore the Green Guide and the e-NetCoM project for related environmental learning.
