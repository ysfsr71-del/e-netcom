School Bike Parking and Active Travel: Start with Safety

A practical school guide to walking and cycling, safe bike parking, inclusive routes and honest impact measurement.

Safety comes before mode shift

Active travel includes walking, cycling and accessible wheeled journeys, not just bicycles. Never recommend a route to children without assessing traffic speed, crossings, footpaths and local conditions. The aim is a safer choice, not pressure to cycle.

Map risks at the school gate

With staff and caregivers, observe vehicle movements during arrival and departure. Record blocked sightlines, interrupted pavements, lighting and crossing points on a simple map. Children must not be sent into traffic to collect data; adults can observe from safe positions.

Design a useful bike parking area

Seek school approval for a visible location close to the entrance without blocking pedestrian flow. Provide sturdy frame-locking points, space to manoeuvre, weather protection where feasible and an accessible route. Never obstruct emergency exits.

Make the programme inclusive

Not every family owns a bicycle or lives near a safe route. Participation must remain voluntary. Consider disabled pupils, those who depend on school transport and families travelling from distant neighbourhoods. Walking or combining public transport with a short walk can also count.

A four-week pilot

Week one: collect an anonymous voluntary travel baseline. Week two: map risks with adults. Week three: discuss feasible improvements with the school and local authority. Week four: communicate the safer options. Follow local road rules and appropriate adult-supervision and helmet guidance.

Measure changes without exaggeration

Compare vehicle counts at the gate and voluntary travel-mode responses before and after the pilot. Do not claim a precise carbon saving merely because more bicycles appear: trip distance, vehicle type and which car journeys were actually replaced are needed for a credible estimate.

Share responsibilities

Street design, speed management and formal road markings belong to competent authorities. Pupils can contribute views through safe classroom activities; they should not carry out traffic engineering. Start with practical changes that can be monitored and maintained.
