Classroom Ventilation and Air Quality Log

Fresh air is an often invisible part of a good learning environment. Smell alone cannot tell us whether a classroom is adequately ventilated. This guide helps students work with teachers and building staff to observe conditions, interpret limited evidence and propose realistic improvements.

Put safety and responsibility first

Windows and mechanical systems must be operated according to school safety rules. Students must not lean out of windows, reach elevated openings or alter ventilation equipment. When outdoor pollution, extreme weather, noise or security create problems, staff should select suitable alternatives.

Map the starting conditions

Record approximate occupancy, lesson duration, operable windows, doors and any visible mechanical ventilation outlets. Window counts alone do not prove an adequate air-exchange rate. Ask staff whether maintenance information or ventilation guidance is available.

Build a simple observation log

For each observation, note date, time, occupancy, window and door position, known mechanical ventilation status and outdoor conditions. Record comfort feedback only in aggregate. Do not create named health-symptom lists; refer health concerns through the school's established procedures.

What if you have a CO₂ monitor?

A carbon dioxide monitor may help reveal trends in crowded indoor spaces, but CO₂ is not a complete measure of air quality or infection risk. Placement, calibration and measurement uncertainty matter. Avoid declaring a room safe or unsafe based on a single reading or universal threshold.

A four-week school project

Week one: observe existing routines. Week two: agree on a safe routine with the teacher. Week three: compare lessons with similar occupancy and weather. Week four: prepare a one-page report for school management. Always explain differences in weather and class size.

Balance fresh air and energy use

Leaving windows open for hours is not automatically best in every season. Consider heating and cooling needs, outdoor pollution and safety together. Only authorized staff should maintain mechanical systems. Staff can assess short, planned airing periods where appropriate.

Share findings responsibly

Do not record student names, faces or individual medical details. Share aggregate occupancy, the number of observations and the changes attempted. Make it clear that a small observational project cannot establish definitive cause and effect.

Classroom checklist

Has an adult taken responsibility? Are outdoor and safety conditions suitable? Was the ventilation method logged? Were monitor limitations documented? Are results anonymous and clear? Have practical recommendations reached the relevant school staff?
